# Business Model Canvas / Lean Canvas

> **Quick Reference**
> - **Core idea**: ビジネスモデルを1ページ9ブロックで可視化する
> - **Key question**: 価値をどう創造し、届け、収益化するか？
> - **When to use**: ビジネスモデルの全体像を整理・共有・比較したいとき
> - **Output**: BMC（既存事業向け）or Lean Canvas（スタートアップ向け）
> - **Time**: 90-120分（初回作成）、30分（更新）

## Overview

The **Business Model Canvas (BMC)** is a strategic management tool created by Alexander Osterwalder that describes how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value through 9 building blocks on a single page. The **Lean Canvas**, adapted by Ash Maurya, replaces 4 of those blocks to focus on problems, solutions, key metrics, and unfair advantages -- making it more suited for early-stage startups operating under high uncertainty.

Both canvases serve the same core purpose: force clarity about your business model in a structured, visual format that fits on one page.

## When to Apply

| Situation | Recommended Canvas |
|---|---|
| Existing business analyzing or communicating its model | BMC |
| Corporate innovation / intrapreneurship | BMC |
| Early-stage startup exploring product-market fit | Lean Canvas |
| Pitching to investors (seed/pre-seed) | Lean Canvas |
| Comparing multiple business model options side by side | Either |
| Pivoting -- documenting before/after | Either |
| Workshop with non-technical stakeholders | BMC |

**Rule of thumb**: If you don't yet know your customer's #1 problem, start with Lean Canvas. If you already have a working business and want to map it, use BMC.

## When NOT to Use

| Situation | Why Not | Better Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| 顧客の課題がまだ分かっていない | Canvasは整理ツールであり、課題発見ツールではない | > **See also**: `jobs-to-be-done/` or `mom-test/` |
| 特定の仮説を検証したい | Canvasは全体像。仮説検証にはテスト設計が必要 | > **See also**: `pretotyping/` or `validation-patterns/` |
| 財務モデルの詳細分析 | Revenue/Cost Structureブロックは概要レベル | > **See also**: `unit-economics/` for LTV/CAC, `tam-sam-som/` for market sizing |
| 完璧な事業計画書を作りたい | Canvasは思考整理ツール。計画書にはならない | Canvasを起点にして事業計画書を別途作成 |

## The 9 Building Blocks

### Business Model Canvas (Osterwalder)

| # | Block | Core Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Segments** | Who are we creating value for? |
| 2 | **Value Propositions** | What value do we deliver to the customer? |
| 3 | **Channels** | How do we reach our customer segments? |
| 4 | **Customer Relationships** | What type of relationship does each segment expect? |
| 5 | **Revenue Streams** | For what value are customers willing to pay? |
| 6 | **Key Resources** | What key resources does our value proposition require? |
| 7 | **Key Activities** | What key activities does our value proposition require? |
| 8 | **Key Partnerships** | Who are our key partners and suppliers? |
| 9 | **Cost Structure** | What are the most important costs inherent in our model? |

### Lean Canvas (Ash Maurya)

| # | Block | Core Question | Replaces (BMC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Problem** | What are the top 3 problems? | Key Partnerships |
| 2 | **Customer Segments** | Who has these problems most acutely? | (same) |
| 3 | **Unique Value Proposition** | Single, clear message that turns a visitor into a customer | (same, narrower) |
| 4 | **Solution** | Top 3 features that solve the problems | Key Resources |
| 5 | **Channels** | How do you reach customers? | (same) |
| 6 | **Revenue Streams** | How do you make money? | (same) |
| 7 | **Cost Structure** | What are your fixed and variable costs? | (same) |
| 8 | **Key Metrics** | What numbers tell you the business is working? | Customer Relationships |
| 9 | **Unfair Advantage** | What cannot be easily copied or bought? | Key Activities |

## Step-by-Step Process

### Phase 1: Preparation (15 min)

1. **Choose the right canvas** based on the situation table above.
2. **Define scope**: Are you mapping the current state ("as-is") or a future state ("to-be")?
3. **Gather participants**: Ideally 3-6 people with diverse perspectives (product, sales, engineering, finance).
4. **Materials**: Large printout or whiteboard, sticky notes, markers. Or a digital tool.

### Phase 2: Filling the Canvas (60-90 min)

**Recommended fill order for BMC:**

1. **Customer Segments** -- Start here. Everything else depends on who you serve.
   - Be specific. "SMBs" is too broad. "SaaS companies with 10-50 employees in Japan" is better.
   - List all segments, then prioritize. Star your primary segment.
   - Consider: Mass market, niche, segmented, multi-sided platform, diversified.

2. **Value Propositions** -- For each segment, what job do you help them do?
   - Use the format: "We help [segment] to [outcome] by [mechanism]."
   - Quantify where possible: "reduce onboarding time by 60%."
   - Types: Newness, performance, customization, design, brand/status, price, cost reduction, risk reduction, accessibility, convenience.
   - > **See also**: `jobs-to-be-done/` -- Value Propositionを深く理解するためのフレームワーク

3. **Channels** -- How does the value reach the customer?
   - Map the full journey: Awareness > Evaluation > Purchase > Delivery > After-sales.
   - Own channels (website, sales team) vs. partner channels (distributors, app stores).

4. **Customer Relationships** -- What does the customer expect from you?
   - Types: Personal assistance, dedicated rep, self-service, automated, communities, co-creation.
   - Consider acquisition vs. retention vs. upselling motives.

5. **Revenue Streams** -- How and how much do customers pay?
   - Types: Asset sale, usage fee, subscription, licensing, brokerage, advertising.
   - Pricing: Fixed (list price, volume-dependent) vs. dynamic (negotiation, auction, yield management).
   - > **See also**: `unit-economics/` -- Revenue StreamsをLTV/CAC/粗利率で深掘り

6. **Key Resources** -- What do you absolutely need?
   - Categories: Physical, intellectual (patents, data), human, financial.

7. **Key Activities** -- What must you do well?
   - Categories: Production, problem-solving, platform/network management.

8. **Key Partnerships** -- Who helps you that isn't on your payroll?
   - Motivations: Optimization, risk reduction, resource acquisition.
   - Types: Strategic alliances, co-opetition, joint ventures, buyer-supplier.

9. **Cost Structure** -- What does all of this cost?
   - Cost-driven vs. value-driven model.
   - Fixed costs, variable costs, economies of scale, economies of scope.

**Recommended fill order for Lean Canvas:**

1. **Problem** (top 3 problems) + **Existing Alternatives** (how people solve it today)
   - > **See also**: `mom-test/` -- 顧客の本当の問題を発見するインタビュー手法
2. **Customer Segments** + **Early Adopters** (the subset who feel the pain most)
3. **Unique Value Proposition** + **High-Level Concept** (e.g., "Uber for X")
4. **Solution** (top 3 features, keep it minimal)
5. **Channels** (path to customers)
6. **Revenue Streams** and **Cost Structure**
7. **Key Metrics** (the one number that matters most right now)
8. **Unfair Advantage** (honest -- if you don't have one yet, leave it blank)

### Phase 3: Stress-Test (30 min)

Ask these questions for each block:

- **Is this a fact or an assumption?** Mark assumptions with a red dot.
- **What's the riskiest assumption?** That's what you validate first.
- **Does this block align with the others?** (e.g., "freemium" revenue model but "high-touch sales" channel is a contradiction)
- **Can a stranger understand this in 2 minutes?** If not, simplify.

> **See also**: `lean-startup/` -- 最もリスクの高い仮説をBuild-Measure-Learnで検証, `pretotyping/` -- 需要仮説の高速検証

### Phase 4: Iterate

- Revisit the canvas every 2-4 weeks (startup) or quarterly (established business).
- Create a new version each time -- don't erase history.
- Name versions: v1, v2, ... or date them.

## BMC vs Lean Canvas: Decision Guide

| Dimension | BMC | Lean Canvas |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | How the business works | What risks to tackle first |
| Audience | Internal teams, corporate boards | Founders, early-stage teams |
| Problem block | Implicit (inside Value Prop) | Explicit (Block #1) |
| Metrics block | Absent | Explicit (Key Metrics) |
| Partnerships | Explicit block | Absent (not a priority yet) |
| Unfair Advantage | Absent | Explicit block |
| Best for | Mapping, communicating, optimizing | Searching, pivoting, validating |
| Iteration speed | Quarterly | Weekly/biweekly |

## Anti-Patterns (これをやったらアウト)

### 1. 右側を無視する（Left-Side Bias）
**Detection**: Key Activities, Key Resources, Key Partnersは詳細に記入されているが、Customer Relationships, Channelsが空欄
**Problem**: 「何を作るか」は詳しいが「どう売るか」が不明。収益は右側にある
**Fix**: 右側（Customer Segments, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams）を先に埋める

### 2. 「全員がターゲット」セグメント
**Detection**: Customer Segmentsに「すべての企業」「日本の中小企業」など広すぎる記述
**Problem**: 全員がターゲット = 誰もターゲットでない。マーケティングもプロダクトも焦点が合わない
**Fix**: 「この人に電話して売れるか？」テスト。具体的な1社・1人をイメージできるまで絞る

### 3. 機能リスト = Value Proposition
**Detection**: 「AI搭載」「クラウド対応」「リアルタイム分析」などの技術的機能が並んでいる
**Problem**: 機能は手段であり価値ではない。顧客が気にするのは結果
**Fix**: 「So what?」テスト。「AI搭載」→「So what?」→「作業時間が50%削減」→ これがValue Proposition

### 4. Canvas完成 = 検証完了
**Detection**: Canvasを一度作って満足し、次のステップに進まない
**Problem**: Canvasは仮説の整理であって検証ではない。全ブロックが仮説（特にスタートアップ）
**Fix**: 各ブロックに「Fact or Assumption?」タグを付け、Assumptionから検証開始

### 5. Unfair Advantageの過大評価（Lean Canvas）
**Detection**: 「優秀なチーム」「先行者利益」「AI技術」をUnfair Advantageに記入
**Problem**: これらはコピー可能。本当のUnfair Advantage: 独自データ、ネットワーク効果、規制上の優位性、構造的コスト優位
**Fix**: 「競合が1億円使っても3年かけても追いつけないか？」テスト。Noなら正直に空欄にする

### 6. Revenue StreamsとPricingの混同
**Detection**: Revenue Streamsに「月額9,800円」と書いてある
**Problem**: Revenue Streamsは収益メカニズム（サブスクリプション、従量課金、ライセンス等）であり、具体的な価格設定ではない
**Fix**: Revenue Streams = 仕組み、Pricing = 具体的数値。Canvasにはメカニズムを記入し、価格分析は別途行う

## Common Pitfalls

1. **"Everything for everyone" segments.** If your Customer Segment says "all businesses," you haven't done the work. Narrow down until it feels uncomfortably specific.

2. **Feature lists as Value Propositions.** "We have AI-powered analytics" is a feature. "30% faster decision-making for marketing teams" is a value proposition.

3. **Ignoring the right side.** Teams love filling Key Activities and Key Resources (what they build) but skip Customer Relationships and Channels (how they sell). The right side is where revenue lives.

4. **Treating the canvas as a one-time exercise.** A canvas is a living document. If yours hasn't changed in 3 months, you're not learning.

5. **Confusing Revenue Streams with pricing.** Revenue Streams = the mechanism (subscription, per-transaction, licensing). Pricing = the specific numbers.

6. **Filling Unfair Advantage too optimistically (Lean Canvas).** "Our team" and "first mover advantage" are rarely unfair advantages. Real unfair advantages: proprietary data, network effects, structural cost advantages, regulatory moats, community.

7. **Not linking blocks.** Each block should logically connect to adjacent blocks. If your Key Activity is "software development" but your Value Proposition is "personal consulting," something is misaligned.

8. **Skipping Existing Alternatives (Lean Canvas).** If you don't know how people solve the problem today, you don't understand the problem. The #1 existing alternative is often "doing nothing" or "using a spreadsheet."
