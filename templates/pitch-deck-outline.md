# Pitch Deck Outline / ピッチデック構成

> 10-12 slides. Each slide has one job. Say less, mean more.
> Total presentation time: 10-20 minutes depending on context.

**Status Markers / ステータスマーカー:**
- `[A]` = Assumption (未検証の仮定)
- `[E]` = Evidence-based (エビデンスあり)
- `[?]` = Unknown (不明 -- 調査が必要)

---

## Slide 1: タイトル / Title

**Purpose:** First impression. Set the tone.

**Key Content:**
- Company name and logo
- One-line description (what you do in plain language): [FILL: one sentence]
- Presenter name and title: [FILL]
- Date: [FILL]

**What investors look for:**
- Clarity -- can they understand what you do in 5 seconds?
- Professional but not over-designed

**Common mistakes:**
- Tagline is too clever or abstract ("Reimagining the future of work")
- Too much text or information
- Missing contact information

---

## Slide 2: 課題 / Problem

**Purpose:** Make the audience feel the pain. If they don't believe the problem is real and painful, nothing else matters.

**Key Content:**
- The problem stated in 1-2 sentences: [FILL: problem statement]
- Who experiences this problem: [FILL: specific segment]
- How painful it is (quantify): [FILL: time wasted, money lost, frequency]
- How they currently deal with it: [FILL: status quo / current alternatives]

**What investors look for:**
- Is this a real problem or a "nice to have"?
- Is the problem big enough to build a business around?
- Do the founders deeply understand the problem (ideally from personal experience)?

**Common mistakes:**
- Problem is too vague or broad ("Communication is broken")
- No evidence the problem exists (just founder's belief)
- Describing a problem nobody is actively trying to solve
- Jumping to the solution too quickly

---

## Slide 3: ソリューション / Solution

**Purpose:** Show how you solve the problem. Keep it simple and concrete.

**Key Content:**
- What your product/service does (not how it works): [FILL: value delivered]
- How it solves the specific problem from Slide 2: [FILL: connection to problem]
- Demo screenshot, product image, or workflow diagram: [FILL: visual asset]
- One clear "aha moment" that shows the value: [FILL: key insight]

**What investors look for:**
- Direct mapping from problem to solution
- Simplicity and elegance
- Evidence it works (demo, testimonials, metrics)

**Common mistakes:**
- Feature dump instead of benefit-driven narrative
- Too technical for the audience
- Solution doesn't clearly connect to the stated problem
- No visual -- just text describing the product

---

## Slide 4: 市場規模 / Market Size

**Purpose:** Show the opportunity is large enough to justify investment.

**Key Content:**
- TAM (Total Addressable Market): [FILL: entire market] [A]/[E]
- SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market): [FILL: segment you can reach] [A]/[E]
- SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): [FILL: 3-5 year capture] [A]/[E]
- Bottom-up calculation: [FILL: methodology]
- Market trends: [FILL: growth drivers]

**What investors look for:**
- Is the market large enough for a venture-scale return?
- Is the market growing?
- Is the bottom-up math credible?
- Does the founder understand their specific slice?

**Common mistakes:**
- Only showing TAM ("It's a $50B market!")
- Top-down only ("If we get just 1% of...")
- No source citations for market data
- Confusing market size with revenue opportunity

---

## Slide 5: ビジネスモデル / Business Model

**Purpose:** Explain how you make money. Be specific.

**Key Content:**
- Revenue model: [FILL: subscription, transaction, marketplace, etc.]
- Pricing: [FILL: actual numbers]
- Unit economics: [FILL: CAC, LTV, LTV/CAC ratio, payback period]
- Key assumptions: [FILL: what these numbers depend on]

**What investors look for:**
- Clear path to revenue
- Healthy unit economics (or clear plan to reach them)
- Pricing validated with real customers
- Scalability of the model

**Common mistakes:**
- "We'll figure out monetization later"
- No actual pricing -- just a model description
- Unit economics that don't work (CAC > LTV)
- Ignoring churn in LTV calculations

---

## Slide 6: トラクション / Traction / Validation

**Purpose:** Show evidence that this is working. The most important slide after Problem.

**Key Content:**
- Key metrics with actual numbers: [FILL: MRR, users, growth rate, retention]
- Growth chart: [FILL: include time axis]
- Key milestones achieved: [FILL: list]
- Customer logos or testimonials (if B2B): [FILL: names/quotes]
- Pre-revenue signals: [FILL: waitlist, LOIs, pre-orders if applicable]

**What investors look for:**
- Month-over-month growth rate
- Retention / engagement (not just signups)
- Evidence of product-market fit signals
- Revenue trajectory

**Common mistakes:**
- Vanity metrics (total signups without active users)
- No time axis on charts
- Cherry-picking a good period
- Confusing "traction" with "activity" (building is not traction; customers using/paying is)

---

## Slide 7: 競合環境 / Competition / Market Landscape

**Purpose:** Show you understand the landscape and can articulate your differentiation.

**Key Content:**
- Competitive landscape: [FILL: 2x2 matrix, comparison table, or positioning map]
- Key competitors and what they do well: [FILL: honest assessment]
- Your differentiation: [FILL: honest and specific]
- Why now: [FILL: timing / what changed]

**What investors look for:**
- Honest acknowledgment of competition (never say "no competitors")
- Clear, defensible differentiation
- Understanding of indirect competitors and substitutes
- Compelling "why now" narrative

**Common mistakes:**
- "We have no competition" (instant credibility killer)
- Feature comparison matrix where you win every row
- Ignoring indirect competitors and the status quo
- Differentiation based on features alone (easily copied)

---

## Slide 8: チーム / Team

**Purpose:** Show you are the right people to solve this problem.

**Key Content:**
- Founders with relevant background: [FILL: why YOU?]
- Key hires and their expertise: [FILL: team composition]
- Advisor/board highlights: [FILL: if noteworthy]
- Founder-market fit story: [FILL: personal connection to the problem]

**What investors look for:**
- Domain expertise relevant to the problem
- Complementary skill sets (technical + business)
- Previous startup or relevant industry experience
- Grit and commitment signals

**Common mistakes:**
- Listing every team member (focus on 2-4 key people)
- Irrelevant credentials (Harvard MBA for a plumbing SaaS)
- No explanation of why this team is uniquely suited
- Missing roles that are clearly needed

---

## Slide 9: 財務予測 / Financials / Projections

**Purpose:** Show you understand the numbers and have a credible plan.

**Key Content:**
- 3-year revenue projection with key assumptions: [FILL: numbers + assumptions]
- Key expense categories: [FILL: major cost items]
- Path to profitability: [FILL: or path to next milestone]
- Current burn rate and runway: [FILL: months remaining]
- Key financial milestones: [FILL: list]

**What investors look for:**
- Are the assumptions reasonable?
- Does the founder understand the drivers of the business?
- Is the growth rate ambitious but credible?
- Clear connection between funding and milestones

**Common mistakes:**
- Hockey stick projections with no basis
- Missing or unrealistic expense assumptions
- Not knowing your current burn rate
- Projections that don't connect to the business model slide

---

## Slide 10: 資金調達 / The Ask

**Purpose:** Be clear about what you need and what you will do with it.

**Key Content:**
- Amount raising: [FILL: specific number]
- Type of round: [FILL: pre-seed, seed, Series A]
- Use of funds: [FILL: specific breakdown -- hiring, product, marketing]
- Key milestones the funding will achieve: [FILL: list]
- Timeline: [FILL: expected duration]

**What investors look for:**
- Reasonable amount for the stage
- Clear use of funds tied to milestones
- Milestones that de-risk the next round
- Terms (if relevant at this stage)

**Common mistakes:**
- Vague use of funds ("growth and development")
- Asking for too much or too little for the stage
- No connection between funding and specific outcomes
- Forgetting to include the actual ask amount

---

## 補足スライド / Optional Slides

### Appendix: Product Demo / Screenshots
- Use only if the product is visually compelling
- Keep it to 2-3 screens maximum

### Appendix: Detailed Financials
- Full P&L projections
- Detailed assumptions
- Scenario analysis

### Appendix: Customer Case Studies / 導入事例
- Specific examples of customer success
- Quantified outcomes

---

## プレゼンチェックリスト / Presentation Checklist

### コンテンツ / Content
- [ ] Every slide has one clear point
- [ ] Problem-Solution connection is obvious
- [ ] All numbers are sourced and defensible
- [ ] Traction data is current (within last month)
- [ ] Ask is specific with clear use of funds
- [ ] All claims tagged with [A] / [E] / [?]

### デザイン / Design
- [ ] No more than 6 lines of text per slide
- [ ] Font size readable from back of room (24pt minimum)
- [ ] Consistent visual style throughout
- [ ] Charts are labeled and easy to read
- [ ] No clip art or stock photos that feel generic

### デリバリー / Delivery
- [ ] Can present in 10 minutes (leave time for Q&A)
- [ ] Practiced the transitions between slides
- [ ] Prepared for likely questions on each slide
- [ ] Know which slides to skip if time is short
- [ ] Have a version for email (more text) vs. presentation (less text)

## この記入テンプレートの使い方 / How to Fill This Template

1. **Start with Problem and Traction**: These are the two slides investors care about most. If these are weak, the rest does not matter.
2. **Fill [A]/[E]/[?] markers**: Before presenting, ensure you know which claims are backed by evidence and which are assumptions.
3. **Prepare two versions**: One for live presentation (less text, more visuals) and one for email (more detail, standalone).
4. **Practice the "so what" test**: For each slide, ask "so what?" -- if the answer is not obvious, the slide needs work.
5. **Use the Presentation Checklist**: Run through it before every pitch.
6. **Iterate after every pitch**: Update based on investor questions and reactions.
