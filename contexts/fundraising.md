# Context: Fundraising Mode

> Preparing for investment, budget approval, or stakeholder buy-in. The narrative must be compelling AND honest.

## Entry Criteria (このモードに入る条件)

- Preparing a pitch deck for investors or VCs
- Writing a business case for internal budget approval
- Seeking grants or government funding (助成金・補助金)
- Any situation where you need to persuade someone to commit resources to your venture
- Have validated evidence (from validation mode) or traction data (from execution mode) to present

## Exit Criteria (このモードを出る条件)

- **-> Execution**: Funding secured -- deploy capital according to the milestones presented to investors
- **-> Validation**: Investor feedback reveals a gap in your evidence -- go back and validate before re-pitching
- **-> Exploration**: Fundraising failed AND investor feedback suggests the market/problem is wrong -- broaden the search
- **-> Back to Fundraising**: Feedback incorporated, materials revised, ready for next round of pitches

## Mindset

Think like a **storyteller with integrity**. Your job is to present the strongest honest case for the business.

- Compelling narrative is essential -- investors buy stories, not spreadsheets
- BUT the intellectual-honesty rule is non-negotiable -- never fabricate or exaggerate
- Anticipate tough questions and prepare honest answers
- The best pitch acknowledges risks and shows you've thought about them

## Behaviors

### Narrative Structure

Every fundraising narrative needs these elements:

```
1. THE PROBLEM (Why this matters)
   - Specific, relatable pain point
   - Quantified impact on the customer
   - Why existing solutions fail

2. THE SOLUTION (What you're building)
   - Clear, simple explanation
   - Demo or prototype if possible
   - Unique insight or approach

3. WHY NOW (Market timing)
   - What changed recently (technology, regulation, behavior)
   - Why this wasn't possible or viable 3 years ago
   - Tailwinds you're riding

4. MARKET SIZE (How big is the opportunity)
   - TAM / SAM / SOM with methodology
   - Bottom-up preferred over top-down
   - Evidence-based tags on all numbers

5. TRACTION (Proof it's working)
   - Metrics, customers, revenue, growth rate
   - Qualitative signals if early stage
   - Honest about the stage you're at

6. BUSINESS MODEL (How you make money)
   - Unit economics (even estimated)
   - Pricing model and rationale
   - Path to profitability

7. TEAM (Why you can execute)
   - Relevant experience and expertise
   - Founder-market fit
   - Key hires needed

8. THE ASK (What you need)
   - Amount and use of funds
   - Milestones the funding enables
   - Expected timeline
```

### Japanese Fundraising Landscape (日本の資金調達環境)

Japan has a distinct fundraising ecosystem with multiple paths beyond traditional VC:

**Venture Capital (VC)**
- Major Japanese VCs: JAFCO, Global Brain, SBI Investment, ANRI, Coral Capital, DNX Ventures
- Japanese VCs typically invest smaller amounts at earlier stages compared to US VCs
- Relationship-building (関係構築) before pitching is more important than in the US
- Expect longer due diligence periods -- 3-6 months is common
- Some VCs specialize in B2B SaaS (e.g., ALL STAR SAAS FUND, DNX) or specific verticals

**Corporate VC / CVC (事業会社CVC)**
- Large Japanese corporations actively invest through CVCs: NTT, KDDI, SoftBank, Toyota, Sony
- CVC investment often comes with strategic partnership or customer relationship
- Approach through the CVC's parent company if you are already a vendor/partner
- CVC deals can take longer due to corporate approval processes but often include commercial agreements

**Government Grants & Subsidies (助成金・補助金)**
- **ものづくり補助金**: Up to 10M+ JPY for manufacturing/service innovation
- **IT導入補助金**: For SMBs adopting IT tools -- your product could be eligible
- **事業再構築補助金**: For business model transformation
- **NEDO (国立研究開発法人)**: Technology development grants, larger amounts
- **J-Startup**: Government acceleration program with visibility and introductions
- **SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research)**: R&D grants for specific government needs
- Grants are non-dilutive but require detailed reporting (実績報告) and specific use of funds
- Application timing is critical -- most programs have fixed application windows

**Other Funding Sources**
- **日本政策金融公庫**: Government-backed loans with favorable terms for startups
- **Angel investors**: Growing but still smaller ecosystem than US -- look for industry-specific angels
- **Crowdfunding**: Makuake, CAMPFIRE for product validation + funding simultaneously
- **Revenue-based financing**: Emerging option for SaaS companies with recurring revenue

**Pitching in Japan**
- Pitch decks for Japanese investors should be more detailed than US versions (15-20 slides acceptable)
- Include an explicit "リスクと対策" (Risks and Mitigation) section -- honesty is valued
- Japanese investors care deeply about team composition and 覚悟 (commitment/determination)
- Prepare both a presentation version and a detailed 事業計画書 (business plan document)
- Follow up with a formal お礼メール after every meeting

### Honesty in Fundraising

Rules are NOT relaxed in fundraising mode. In fact, they are more important:

- **Do NOT inflate market size** -- investors will check. Getting caught destroys credibility.
- **Do NOT hide risks** -- present them proactively with mitigation plans
- **Do NOT project hockey-stick growth without justification** -- show the assumptions behind projections
- **Do NOT claim "no competition"** -- there is always competition, even if indirect. Show you understand the landscape.
- **Do state what you DON'T know** -- investors respect self-awareness

```
[FUNDRAISING HONESTY CHECK]
Before finalizing any pitch material, verify:
- [ ] All market numbers have tagged evidence levels
- [ ] Projections show assumptions and sensitivity ranges
- [ ] Risks are presented (not hidden)
- [ ] "We don't know yet" appears where appropriate
- [ ] No vanity metrics presented as meaningful traction
```

### Devil's Advocate: Investor Tough Questions

In fundraising mode, the devil's advocate simulates investor due diligence. Prepare answers for:

**The Big Five:**

1. **"Why now?"**
   - What market shift makes this timely?
   - Why didn't someone build this 5 years ago? Why won't it be too late in 2 years?

2. **"Why you?"**
   - What unfair advantage does your team have?
   - Why can't a better-funded team copy this?

3. **"What if [BigCo] does this?"**
   - How do you compete against Google/Amazon/Microsoft/incumbent?
   - What is your defensible moat?

4. **"What are the biggest risks?"**
   - Be specific: technical, market, regulatory, team
   - Show mitigation strategies for each

5. **"What does failure look like?"**
   - What assumptions, if wrong, would kill this?
   - At what point would you shut down or pivot?

**Additional tough questions to prepare:**

- "Your CAC seems high -- how does this improve with scale?"
- "What's your churn rate and why?"
- "How did you arrive at this valuation?"
- "What happens if you raise half of what you're asking for?"
- "Who are your first 10 customers and why did they buy?"
- "What did you learn from your failures / pivots?"

### Financial Projections

Financial projections in fundraising must include:

```
## Financial Projections (3-year)

### Assumptions (MUST be explicit)
- Customer growth rate: X% monthly [ASSUMPTION: based on similar companies]
- Churn rate: Y% monthly [ESTIMATE: based on early data]
- ARPU: $Z [FACT: current pricing / ASSUMPTION: if pre-revenue]
- CAC: $W [ESTIMATE: based on test campaigns]

### Scenarios
| Metric | Conservative | Base | Optimistic |
|--------|-------------|------|-----------|
| Year 1 Revenue | $X | $Y | $Z |
| Year 2 Revenue | $X | $Y | $Z |
| Year 3 Revenue | $X | $Y | $Z |
| Break-even | Month X | Month Y | Month Z |

### Sensitivity Analysis
- If churn is 2x higher: [impact]
- If CAC is 2x higher: [impact]
- If ARPU is 50% lower: [impact]
```

Never present only the optimistic scenario. Always show the range.

## Time Box

- **Pitch deck creation**: 1-2 weeks
- **Financial model**: 3-5 days
- **Investor Q&A prep**: 2-3 days
- **Rehearsal and refinement**: 1 week
- **Total fundraising prep**: 3-4 weeks
- **Grant/subsidy applications**: 2-4 weeks (align with application windows)

## Rules Still Active

All rules remain active at full intensity. Fundraising is where the temptation to bend the truth is strongest, which is exactly why the rules matter most:

- **intellectual-honesty.md**: NON-NEGOTIABLE. A dishonest pitch will fail in due diligence or, worse, succeed and create unsustainable expectations.
- **evidence-based.md**: Tag every claim. Investors will verify.
- **customer-first.md**: The best pitch is built on real customer evidence.
- **cost-conscious.md**: Show investors you understand unit economics and capital efficiency.
- **time-boxing.md**: Fundraising itself has a time box. Don't let it consume the entire business.
