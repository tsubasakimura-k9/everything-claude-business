# Context: Exploration Mode

> Early stage. The goal is to discover opportunities, not to commit to one. Divergent thinking takes priority.

## Entry Criteria (このモードに入る条件)

- Starting a new business or product idea from scratch
- Entering an unfamiliar market or domain
- "I have a vague sense of an opportunity but nothing concrete"
- Annual or quarterly strategy review -- "where should we look next?"
- Previous initiative was killed and you need to find the next thing
- Returning from a failed validation -- need to widen the search again

## Exit Criteria (このモードを出る条件)

- **-> Validation**: You have 2-3 opportunities in Tier 1 with strong problem signals AND at least one target customer segment identified for each
- **-> Fundraising**: You need funding to even begin validation (rare -- most validation should be cheap)
- **-> Back to Exploration**: N/A (you are already here)

## Mindset

Think like a **scout**, not a soldier. Your job is to map the terrain, not to conquer it.

- Cast a wide net before narrowing down
- Quantity of ideas matters more than quality at this stage
- "Interesting" is a valid reason to explore further
- Kill nothing prematurely, but flag obvious fatal flaws (per intellectual-honesty rule)

## Behaviors

### Prioritize Breadth Over Depth

- Explore 5-10 opportunity areas before deep-diving into any single one
- For each area, produce a quick scan (30 minutes max), not a full analysis
- Use the format below for rapid opportunity assessment

### Rapid Opportunity Assessment Format

For each opportunity area:

```
## Opportunity: [Name]

**Problem signal**: [What makes you think this is a real problem?]
**Who has it**: [Rough target segment]
**How they solve it today**: [Current alternatives]
**Why now**: [What changed to create this opportunity?]
**Quick size estimate**: [Order of magnitude — $1M? $100M? $1B?]
**Obvious risks**: [Top 2-3 concerns]

Verdict: [Worth exploring / Interesting but unclear / Likely dead end]
```

### Japanese Market Exploration Patterns (日本市場の探索パターン)

When exploring opportunities in the Japanese market, leverage these channels:

**Industry Events & Exhibitions (展示会・業界イベント)**
- Japan IT Week, CEATEC, AI Expo -- observe what problems exhibitors are solving
- Industry-specific exhibitions (e.g., manufacturing: JIMTOF, retail: リテールテック)
- Look for booths with long lines -- they signal unmet demand

**Industry Associations (業界団体)**
- Check white papers and reports from relevant associations (e.g., JISA for IT services, JEITA for electronics)
- Attend open seminars -- great for understanding industry-wide pain points
- Build relationships with association staff who see trends across member companies

**Government Reports & Policy (経産省・政府レポート)**
- METI (経産省) publishes annual reports on digital transformation gaps -- "DXレポート" series
- Check 補助金 and 助成金 programs -- they reveal where the government sees market failures
- IPA (情報処理推進機構) reports on IT talent shortages and technology adoption
- "Society 5.0" and "デジタル田園都市国家構想" policy documents signal where funding will flow

**Japan-Specific Opportunity Signals**
- Aging workforce (高齢化) creating automation demand in traditional industries
- "2025 Cliff" (2025年の崖) -- legacy system migration urgency
- Labor shortage (人手不足) in specific sectors -- construction, logistics, nursing
- Regulatory changes (e.g., electronic invoicing mandate インボイス制度)
- Japanese companies often adopt solutions 2-5 years after US/EU -- look for proven models abroad entering Japan

**Cultural Considerations for Exploration**
- Japanese businesses value 実績 (track record) -- look for problems where you can demonstrate small wins
- Word-of-mouth through trusted networks (紹介) is stronger than cold outreach
- Trade press (日経BP, ITmedia, etc.) articles reveal what decision-makers are reading about

### Recommended Skills & Agents to Use

- **Market researcher**: Scan market reports, trends, and adjacent markets
- **Competitor analyst**: Map existing solutions in each opportunity area
- **Customer profiler**: Build rough personas for potential target segments

### Devil's Advocate Intensity: REDUCED

In exploration mode, the inner critic runs at lower intensity:

- Still flag fatal flaws (per intellectual-honesty rule -- this is non-negotiable)
- Do NOT nitpick individual ideas -- it's too early for that
- Challenge the overall portfolio: "Are you exploring diverse enough areas?"
- Save deep criticism for validation mode

### Output: Opportunity Landscape

The deliverable from exploration mode is an **opportunity landscape**, not a decision:

```
## Opportunity Landscape -- [Domain/Theme]

### Tier 1: Worth Validating (strong signals)
1. [Opportunity A] -- [one-line summary]
2. [Opportunity B] -- [one-line summary]

### Tier 2: Interesting, Needs More Signal
3. [Opportunity C] -- [one-line summary]
4. [Opportunity D] -- [one-line summary]

### Tier 3: Likely Dead End (but noted)
5. [Opportunity E] -- [reason for skepticism]

### Recommended Next Step
Pick 1-2 from Tier 1 and move to Validation mode.
Time box: [X days] for this exploration phase.
```

## Time Box

Exploration mode should not last indefinitely.

- **Default**: 1-2 weeks for a new domain
- **Quick scan**: 2-3 days for a focused area
- **Exit criteria**: You have 2-3 opportunities in Tier 1 worth validating

At the end of the time box, pick and move to Validation mode. Do not stay in exploration mode because "there might be more opportunities." There always will be.
